Love on the Plate · redesign study · 2026-09-20

Four directions for the same app.

Every feature you already have is preserved in all four: the four gallery views, the immersive feed, the photo sheet with its swipe and bilingual copy, search with its four field filters, the analytics dashboard, the upload flow with AI descriptions, magic-link login, both themes, and the mobile-first layout that still behaves on a desktop.

What changes is the visual language — and with it, what the app feels like it is for. The files are built from one shared brief: same 12 dishes, same bilingual descriptions, same analytics numbers, same interactions, same accessibility floor. Only the design differs, so the comparison is honest.

The fourth is a colour study rather than a new structure. Bonheur is Plate Stack, markup for markup, recoloured from a Matisse painting — so you can judge a palette without relearning a layout. Read 03 and 04 as a pair; everything that differs between them is colour.

Read the previews as posters, not screenshots. Each card below embeds the real file, scaled down. Open the full mockup to scroll it, switch themes, change view, open a dish, and resize the device frame yourself — that is where these directions actually differ.

The four directions

Each one is a complete replacement, not a layer on top of the current design. They disagree about almost everything: whether photos or typography lead, whether the app is bright or dark by default, whether it whispers or shouts. 03 and 04 agree about all of that and disagree only about colour — those two are meant to be read side by side.

How Bonheur's palette was derived

Every colour in 04 is measured from mockups/joy_of_life.jpg — Matisse, Bonheur de Vivre, 1906. Clustering plus a hue/chroma histogram, over the canvas region only (x 272–1728, y 700–1840 of the 2000×2500 scan). Nothing was eyeballed, and no hue was invented.

What the recolour did and did not change. 03 and 04 were audited together, in both themes, by walking every rendered text element across all 20 device frames and measuring its computed colour against its nearest painted background. The failing pairs come out identical, element for element — light: none; dark: four, in the upload drop-zone (#E5CE7C on #BDB49F ×21 while dragging, and its #2A2E44 on #12172F ×17 help line, plus #E5CE7C on #F6ECD9 ×1 for Cancel drop) and the search result chip (#E4744A on #F6ECD9 ×18). Those are Plate Stack's debts, not the palette's, and they are reported rather than quietly patched — patching them here would make 03 and 04 differ in more than colour, which is the one thing this pair is for.

How to choose

They are not four intensities of one idea. They optimise for different things, and picking badly costs real work later. Since Plate Stack is the structure you have settled on, the live question is the last card: 03's own palette, or 04's.

Pick 01 if

The writing matters as much as the photo

You read the descriptions, you cook from the archive, and you browse on a laptop as often as on a phone. This is the direction that treats 214 plates as a body of work rather than a feed.

Pick 02 if

The photos are the whole point

This is a gallery of food someone cooked with love, viewed one-handed on a phone. Dark canvas plus a full-bleed, scroll-snapped feed is the strongest possible presentation of that, and it grows naturally out of the dark theme you already have. It is also the direction most likely to feel genuinely new to you — while staying usable, because the editorial structure is still there in the grid and timeline views.

Pick 03 if · your pick, original palette

You want it to feel like yours, loudly

If the gallery is really a shared family thing — read on a kitchen tablet, shown to guests, added to by more than one person — the bento board and scoreboard stats carry that better than either quiet direction. It is the highest-personality option and the most work to keep coherent. Choose this over 04 if the tomato-and-cobalt voltage is the part you like.

Pick 04 if · same structure, quieter

You like the board, not the volume

Identical markup and behaviour to 03, with every colour sampled from Matisse's Bonheur de Vivre. Choose it if the bento clarity is right but the shouting is not: it reads calmer in daylight, gentler on a dark screen, and it already has a whole technique behind it, which means future additions — a new badge, a new chart series — have a rule to follow rather than a vibe to match.

All four were designed against your existing structure, so none of them requires new features. The work is in the token layer and the components.
